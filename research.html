Research

Overview

Dr. Sun investigates how chemical and microbial processes govern the fate, transformation, and impacts of anthropogenic chemicals and materials across natural and engineered systems. By integrating mechanistic studies with quantitative measurements, he seeks to understand environmental persistence and biological interactions, while extending these principles to controlled waste transformation and resource cycling.

Current and future research spans aquatic plastic pollution, contaminants of emerging concern, microbial biogeochemistry, quantitative methods and instrumentation, and sustainable waste-to-resource technologies. Together, these efforts connect fundamental understanding of environmental transformations with the development of quantitative tools and sustainable solutions.

Fate, Transformation, and Persistence

Anthropogenic chemicals and materials undergo chemical, photochemical, physical, and microbial transformations that determine their persistence in the environment. We investigate the mechanisms and environmental factors controlling these processes, with a particular focus on plastics, plastic-associated chemicals, and contaminants of emerging concern.

Environmental Impacts

Environmental transformation can alter the bioavailability and biological effects of chemicals and materials. We investigate how materials and their transformed products, particularly those derived from plastics, influence microbial communities, biogeochemical cycling, and ecological responses.

Quantitative Methods and Instrumentation

Quantifying plastic degradation requires robust methods across material sizes and environmentally relevant conditions. We develop experimental platforms, analytical methods, and quantitative frameworks to measure and characterize the degradation and transformation of macro- and microplastics.

Waste Valorization and Circularity

Chemical and microbial transformation processes can also be harnessed to recover value from waste. We investigate controlled biological processes for converting plastic and other waste-derived carbon into value-added chemicals, materials, and energy, connecting environmental transformation science with circular resource utilization.
