Research

Overview

Dr. Sun investigates how chemical and microbial processes govern the fate, transformation, and impacts of anthropogenic chemicals and materials across natural and engineered systems. By integrating mechanistic studies with quantitative measurements, he seeks to understand environmental persistence and biological interactions, while extending these principles to controlled waste transformation and resource cycling.

Fate, Transformation, and Persistence

What determines whether an anthropogenic chemical or material persists for days, years, or decades in the environment? This research resolves the chemical, photochemical, physical, and microbial mechanisms that govern transformation pathways and rates. Particular emphasis is placed on how environmental conditions and material properties interact with microbial activity to control degradation, transformation products, and environmental lifetimes.

Environmental and Health-Relevant Impacts

Environmental transformation can fundamentally change the behavior and consequences of a contaminant or material. This research connects fate and transformation pathways to bioavailability, biological responses, ecological effects, and health-relevant exposure potential, with the goal of determining when environmental transformation attenuates, preserves, or enhances impacts.

Quantitative Methods and Instrumentation

Understanding environmental transformations requires measurements that bridge controlled experiments and environmentally relevant conditions. I develop and apply experimental platforms, analytical approaches, and quantitative frameworks to resolve biodegradation kinetics, respiration, carbon conversion, transformation rates, and biological responses. These measurements provide the mechanistic and quantitative basis for predicting environmental persistence and transformation.

Waste Valorization and Circularity

The same chemical and microbial processes responsible for environmental degradation can be investigated under controlled conditions to understand carbon conversion and resource recovery from waste streams. This research focuses on the pathways, microbial functions, and process conditions governing the conversion of waste-derived carbon into value-added chemicals, materials, and energy, connecting environmental transformation science with circular carbon utilization.
